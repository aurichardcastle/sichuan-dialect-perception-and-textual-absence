Cohen's kappa was not computed for this study. The AI-human coding workflow does not constitute two independent coders in the traditional sense: the AI functioned as a coding instrument under researcher supervision, not as an autonomous second rater. Reporting a kappa coefficient would misrepresent the procedure as a conventional dual-coder design. See Section 3, Inter-Rater Reliability, for the full methodological rationale.

### Verification Procedure

Coding operated in three layers:

1. **AI first pass.** The large-language-model tool (Claude, Anthropic) proposed candidate codes for each line of each song, grounded in directly quoted lyric text. Every proposed code included a verbatim textual warrant and a classification rationale.

2. **Researcher review.** The researcher reviewed every proposed code, accepting, rejecting, or reclassifying each item. Ambiguous items were flagged as UNCERTAIN rather than forced into a confirmed or rejected category. The researcher exercised final judgment on all coding decisions.

3. **Native Mandarin speaker verification.** All Category 1 (dialect feature) codes were additionally reviewed by a native Mandarin speaker with familiarity with Sichuan dialect, who confirmed or challenged the researcher's dialect-versus-colloquial distinctions. Interview translations were similarly verified.

No code reached confirmed status without passing all three layers.

Across the 40-song corpus (20 Chengdu, 20 comparison):

- **26 confirmed Category 1 (Sichuan dialect) features** were identified across the Chengdu corpus. The comparison corpus produced zero.
- **8 items were flagged UNCERTAIN** during the AI first pass and retained as unresolved after researcher review. All eight fell within Category 1 and involved cases where the boundary between Sichuan dialect and colloquial Mandarin could not be definitively resolved:

### Limitations of the Verification Approach

This layered verification procedure does not eliminate the possibility of systematic bias. The researcher and AI may share blind spots, particularly regarding dialect features that are phonologically realized in performance but orthographically invisible -- which is precisely the phenomenon this study identifies as its central finding. A native Sichuan speaker conducting the same analysis from the outset might identify additional dialect features, particularly subtle lexical choices, pragmatic particles, or discourse markers that an L2 Mandarin speaker would not recognize. This limitation, if operative, would strengthen rather than weaken the central finding: any uncounted dialect features would increase the gap between textual representation and actual dialectal content.
