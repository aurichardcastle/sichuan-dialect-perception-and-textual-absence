**Location:** Street performance area near Chunxi Road (春熙路)

**Observation:** Encountered a small group of young performers doing freestyle rap outside a shopping center. The crowd was mostly university-age. The performers switched between Mandarin and what I now recognize as Sichuan dialect -- the crowd responded more enthusiastically (cheering, repeating lines) during dialect sections. Could not follow all the dialect content at my Mandarin level but the audience engagement shift was visible.

**Reflection:** This was the first time I noticed that dialect seemed to function as a kind of crowd activation -- the switch to dialect created a moment of shared recognition. At the time I did not have the theoretical language for this, but in retrospect this aligns with Bucholtz and Hall's adequation: the dialect created a moment of shared identity between performer and audience that standard Mandarin did not produce as intensely.

### Field Note 4 -- July 22, 2025, Chengdu

**Location:** Walking through the city, general observations

**Observation:** Chengdu is a modern, digitally sophisticated city -- power banks for rent on every corner, e-bikes everywhere, digital payment for everything, facial recognition at MRT gates. Artists use dialect not as a retreat from the modern but as a way of asserting local identity within it.

**Reflection:** This contradicts any framing of dialect use as backward-looking nostalgia. Chengdu's hip-hop scene is thoroughly modern and commercially engaged. Dialect use is a forward-looking creative strategy that asserts regional distinctiveness within China's modern cultural landscape.
